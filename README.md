# on the subject of attribution

i been writing software for a long time, and i have been using tooling for that
since before anyone had any kind of opinion more exciting than "emacs is
actually not an editor, it's an operating system."

i have been using software you might call "ai" for at least a few years. the
only thing that was controversial about this when i started was that i cared
about this at all, and i went to pains to explain which software had been
authored with the help of ai. people thought i was crazy for saying that, and i
did it anyway, and i moved on with my life.

and then a bunch of people decided that they wanted to know for-sure who was
doing it and who was pure.

i fucking hate purity tests. it's always bullshit. if i write bad code, i write
bad code. if i write good code, same deal. but nobody is going to tell me that i
write bad code, or it's not good enough, because i use vim or because i use
goland, or because i don't like light mode, or because i use one ai product or
the next.

please and most sincerely i never want to hear about it ever again, because it
actually makes it difficult for me to write code. i sit here at my terminal,
writing code because i am a programmer, and in the back of my mind, i am
thinking, "will i be pilloried for wasting all the water on the planet or
stealing the works of honest hard-working teenage deviantart artists?"

and the answer, as with all purity tests is, "yeah you'll probably be pilloried
by a person whose opinion you don't give a shit about."

a hundred thousand lines or so ago, a person i am working with had some urgent
concerns about a license or i honestly don't even remember what. and i was
perplexed by the seriousness of the tone. i had this to say.

    if something crashes trying to read our endpoint, that's fucking stupid
    and the service should be fixed. don't be hysterical. we're not building
    critical infrastructure here, we're building a tui that has animated fucking
    characters being silly.

and i have to say i forgot this, everyone who heard this did, and it got kind of
unpleasant to write software because i was, and everyone was, afraid of
innumerable little things like whether the attribution was right, or the
compatibility between to licenses, and so on. there are much bigger, more
importantt things to worry about, like the best way to format a man page that
starts with elaborate ascii art. let it go.

so. i'm going to be over here writing code. i will sign my code, as i always do,
and i can and will change the way that i do this, and i might even stop doing
that. but i am not going to lose sleep over making other people comfortable
about my means of writing software. if you do not want my code in your project,
you are free to reject my diff on that basis. if you wish to reject it on the
basis of the quality of the code, that is a much more normal reason to reject
code, and i would prefer you say something like "your mother smells of
elderberry" to soften the blow. that's how programming works. we fight
ideological wars all the time,  and it's over stupid shit. that's the point.
when programming becomes a problem conflated with the end of the species, that's
when it is very clear that we have lost the plot, and there are too many people
in the room with too many opinions and some of them should, i say this most
sincerely, should please shut the fuck up and go back to programming.

    jane michelle arc, 2026, the year that programming stopped being fun.
    2026 is also the year of the linux desktop
